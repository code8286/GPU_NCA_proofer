\begin{abstract}
Can changing the topology of a neural cellular automaton improve the
initialization from which a transformer learns language? We formulate this as
a comparison of synthetic pre-pre-training distributions followed by the same
language optimization problem. We derive the equivalence between a grid neural
rule and typed message passing, identify what degree-preserving rewiring does
and does not preserve, bound deterministic trajectory entropy, and derive
exact serialization-lag statistics for the binary pilot. We then prove a
conditional descent criterion for backbone transfer with embedding reset,
solve a quadratic model of subsequent convergence, give counterexamples to
unconditional improvement, and calculate when language-stage savings exceed
synthetic preparation costs. All numerical examples are substitutions into
displayed equations or explicit evaluations of closed-form expressions.
No simulation, model training, or fitted convergence curve is used to establish
these results. The note supplies mathematical support and falsifiable conditions;
it does not prove that the project's graph distribution improves real language
models.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Scope and provenance.}
This is a theoretical working document, not a new empirical benchmark or a
claim of novelty for standard mathematical tools. General statements are
proved under stated assumptions. Numerical specialization to the binary
pilot uses its preserved configuration and source identity.
Categorical and stochastic extensions are distinguished explicitly.
The prior GPU pilot is used only for clearly labeled arithmetic checks on
reported measurements.
